## Operating rule

No implementation agent should “improve the paper” globally.

Every intervention must be traceable to one or more ledger items below.

Allowed action labels:

- **KEEP** — content is substantively useful and should survive.
- **REFRAME** — preserve the evidence/content, change the claim or argumentative role.
- **EVIDENCE** — claim may survive only if documentary/analytical support is added.
- **COMPRESS** — retain only what is necessary for the locked argument.
- **DELETE** — remove because it creates burden without advancing the locked argument.
- **VERIFY** — source, quotation, chronology, or attribution must be checked before submission.

Priority:
- **P0** = publication blocker
- **P1** = major revision
- **P2** = important but bounded
- **P3** = submission/polish

Status:
- **RESOLVED**
- **PARTIALLY RESOLVED**
- **UNRESOLVED**
- **NEW RISK**

# C. Section-by-section intervention map

## Abstract

**Objective:** state one primary historical claim, one secondary theoretical claim, one contemporary ROPE payoff.

Required:
- retain institutionalization of survey-based CU;
- retain shift from slack interpretation to inflation-constraint interpretation;
- remove or soften direct “used to discipline distributive conflict” unless evidence is strengthened;
- avoid claiming the paper explains the entire collapse of American Keynesianism.

Pass condition:
- every sentence maps to evidence in the body;
- no concept appears only in the abstract/conclusion.

## 1. Introduction

**KEEP**
- the question of how survey-based routines became authoritative and were repurposed;
- treatment of indicators as contested measurement infrastructure;
- recent CU/theory literature;
- concise method statement.

**REFRAME**
- make the utilization controversy a front-end payoff, not a late conclusion;
- define “ideological embeddedness”;
- define the unit of “conversion/redeployment”;
- add a compact theory–measurement–institution–policy bridge.

**DELETE/COMPRESS**
- duplicate roadmap;
- broad claims about class discipline not yet demonstrated.

Pass condition: by the end of the introduction, a skeptical ROPE reader can identify the explanandum, mechanism, primary contribution, secondary contribution, evidence base, and limits of the claim.

## 2. Pre-Keynesian measurement / Brookings

**KEEP**
- theoretical/practical capacity distinctions;
- measurement methods;
- contemporary reviews;
- evidence of idle capacity.

**REFRAME**
- avoid teleology (“foreshadowed Keynes”) unless carefully phrased;
- make Brookings relevant because it establishes the measurement problem and operational alternatives.

**BOUNDED ADDITION**
- enough institutional background on Brookings/Nourse to answer Referee 1 without creating a biography.

Pass condition: section ends with a precise inherited measurement problem that the postwar regime will solve institutionally.

## 3. Rise of American Keynesianism / pedagogy-policy corridor

**KEEP**
- Hansen/Samuelson only where they connect stabilization theory to demand for operational slack indicators.

**COMPRESS**
- textbook internationalization unless indispensable;
- repeated feedback-loop language;
- generic intellectual history not tied to measurement.

Pass condition: the section explains why a demand-management regime needed an operational indicator of slack; it does not try to retell the whole rise of American Keynesianism.

## 3.2 Survey infrastructure / institutionalization

**PROTECT AS EMPIRICAL CORE**

**KEEP**
- McGraw–Hill survey design;
- Fed embedding;
- contemporary methodological critiques;
- TTP, EMU, AWW alternatives;
- conceptual differences among measures.

**REFRAME**
- “not empirical but political”;
- “politically legible” if unsupported;
- any claim that technical alternatives were unambiguously superior.

**VERIFY**
- Klein & David attribution;
- chronology and exact nature of Fed use/adoption.

Pass condition: this section alone makes a publishable contribution to the institutional history of CU measurement.

## 4. Crisis / recoding

**MAJOR RECONSTRUCTION ZONE**

The section must be rebuilt around the measurement transition.

Core sequence:
1. crisis context creates pressure on the meaning of slack/capacity;
2. specific economists/policy institutions contest what counts as “capacity”;
3. Klein/Greenspan episode demonstrates altered interpretive criterion;
4. new classical/rules-oriented macroeconomics changes the conceptual status of constraints;
5. official/policy use of CU is shown to shift where evidence permits;
6. bounded political implication: inflation constraint increasingly limits full-employment reasoning.

**COMPRESS or DELETE unless directly linked to CU**
- long regulation-theory exposition;
- Great Society/social reproduction detour;
- civil-rights architecture;
- broad Bretton Woods narrative;
- gold-price figure;
- global fiat-dollar argument;
- generalized de-democratization narrative.

Pass condition: at least 70% of the section directly concerns capacity/utilization measurement, interpretation, statistical practice, or policy use.

## 5. Conclusion

**Primary contribution**
Historical-institutional construction and changing interpretation of CU.

**Secondary contribution**
Continuity/discontinuity in technocratic macroeconomic governance.

**ROPE payoff**
Official CU series are not neutral proxies; theoretical/econometric disputes must attend to how measures were institutionally constructed.

**Implication**
Measurement pluralism/democratic governance may be raised cautiously.

**DELETE as independent contribution claims**
- decolonizing economics;
- dependency theory;
- racialized political economy;
- global financial discipline;
unless they have become part of the actual evidence base in the revised body.

Pass condition: no major concept, literature, or normative agenda is introduced for the first time in the conclusion.

# E. Stop conditions

Stop implementation and return to editorial review if any of the following occurs:

1. A revision requires introducing a new explanatory framework not already authorized in the architecture lock.
2. The agent cannot identify direct evidence for a P0 causal/intentional claim.
3. A repair to Section 4 increases rather than decreases its number of independent causal mechanisms.
4. The conclusion begins claiming a field contribution not established in the introduction/body.
5. “Institutional conversion,” “recoding,” “redeployment,” or “same apparatus” are being used with different meanings.
6. New references are proposed as substitutes for clarifying the existing argument rather than to fill a specific evidence gap.
7. The manuscript exceeds the ROPE hard word limit after revision.
