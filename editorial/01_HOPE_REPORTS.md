# 2. Referee 1 — principal assessment

Referee 1 regarded the paper as:

- interesting;
- ambitious;
- potentially publishable after major revision.

The central criticism was that the manuscript simultaneously pursued:

1. socio-technical translation;
2. institutional history;
3. intellectual history;
4. Marxist regulation theory.

The referee argued that any one could sustain a compelling paper, but pursuing all simultaneously stretched the argument too thin.

## Key criticisms

### A. Narrative cohesion
- actors and institutions entered too abruptly;
- insufficient historical, political, and institutional background;
- Brookings and Nourse needed fuller contextualization;
- transitions from pre-Keynesianism to Keynesianism and from Keynesianism to neoliberalism were insufficiently explained;
- the role of capacity-utilization measurement in those transitions remained unclear.

### B. Argument identity
The manuscript shifted between:
- an internalist account of knowledge infrastructure;
- an externalist account of neoliberal instrumentalization.

The referee suggested that the shift itself might be the argument, but insisted that this would need to be explicit in the abstract and introduction.

### C. Evidence and institutions
The paper discussed economists and knowledge production more successfully than:
- institutional embedding;
- daily routines;
- social action;
- translation into governance.

### D. Literature
The referee found the engagement with:
- history of economics;
- sociology/history of economic governance;
- STS;
to be too thin.

### E. Strongest material
The referee explicitly identified the **survey approach** as:
- better executed;
- the manuscript's most significant contribution;
- the strongest candidate around which to reconceive the paper.

This is a binding signal for the ROPE revision.

---

# 3. Referee 2 — principal assessment

Referee 2 reconstructed two major layers in the paper:

1. changes in the understanding and measurement of capacity utilization corresponded to:
   - the move from Keynesian demand management to neoliberal management;
   - the ideological use of economic concepts and measurement in relation to class projects;

2. the neoclassical synthesis contained important continuities with the macroeconomic apparatus that later displaced it.

The referee's positive assessment was unusually strong.

They described the manuscript as:

- an exemplary study in the political economy of economic ideas;
- powerful in relating conceptual change to practical use;
- sophisticated;
- largely convincing;
- non-reductionist in not treating economists as mere expressions of class interest.

These strengths should be preserved.

---

# 4. Referee 2 — principal weaknesses

## A. Missing theoretical preliminaries
The manuscript needed a clearer account of the links among:

- economic theory;
- economic measurement;
- regimes of state intervention;
- class politics.

## B. Regulation-theory burden
The referee questioned what was gained by making Fordism/post-Fordism the main explanatory framework.

The concern was not ideological disagreement with regulation theory.

The concern was explanatory economy:
- the framework imposed additional burdens;
- many broader approaches agree on key features of the 1970s transition;
- the paper could make a more general political-economy argument without having to prove the entire regulation-theory account.

## C. Asymmetry between Keynesian rise and post-1974 transformation
The manuscript needed to explain:
- the technocratic character of the Samuelsonian postwar order;
- its relation to the postwar class compromise;
- how the rise of Keynesian management mirrored the later transition being analyzed.

## D. Economists as agents
The referee asked how economists should be understood as historical agents.

They detected an implicit model in which:
- economic ideas are generated independently;
- political actors later select useful ideas for ideological purposes.

This model was not sufficiently theorized.

## E. Continuity claim
The argument that the post-1974 regime remained continuous with postwar technocratic orthodoxy was regarded as mostly asserted rather than demonstrated.

## F. Central measurement transition underdeveloped
For an article allegedly about capacity utilization, the actual reconstruction of the shift from:
- "thermostatic" demand-management indicator
to
- "inflation sentinel"
was too short and underdeveloped.

The referee requested more economics of the transition, particularly:
- Sargent;
- Lucas;
- the conceptual relationship between capacity, potential output, output gaps, and inflationary limits.

## G. Greenspan interpretation
The referee warned that the Greenspan quotation was being made to carry a stronger argument than the quotation itself established.

## H. Labor-discipline claim
The referee explicitly stated that the paper had **not demonstrated** that capacity utilization became an instrument of labor discipline.

## I. Agency in "recoding"
The referee objected that passive formulations avoided the question:
> Who did the recoding?

This remains a core repair requirement.

# 5. Referee 2 — terminology and prose issues

Specific concerns included:

- inconsistent use of "capacity" versus "capacity utilization";
- confusion between capacity, market-clearing output, and output gaps;
- very long non-parallel sentences;
- opaque contribution statements;
- occasional incorrect or awkward word choice;
- normative ideas introduced in the conclusion without earlier development.

Most of these are now secondary to the architectural repairs.

# 6. What has been repaired in the October 6, 2026 version

The current manuscript has made meaningful progress:

- the introduction now states a clearer research question;
- STS and regulation theory are explicitly demoted from explanatory architecture to contextual background;
- Hansen/Samuelson provide a more symmetrical account of postwar technocracy;
- the survey/Federal Reserve institutional history is substantially more developed;
- Lucas and Sargent are now explicitly discussed;
- the manuscript is clearer about the distinction between measurement and theory.

These are real improvements and should not be undone.

---

# 7. What remains unresolved

The following HOPE concerns remain active:

1. **Agency** — who recoded what, through which institutional action?
2. **Labor discipline** — still asserted more strongly than demonstrated.
3. **Continuity** — "same toolkit" remains too abstract.
4. **Central transition evidence** — the 1970s reinterpretation is still thinner than the earlier historical reconstruction.
5. **Regulation-theory burden** — reduced in the introduction but expanded again in Section 4.
6. **Contribution sprawl** — the conclusion now adds utilization controversy, historical political economy, decolonizing economics, dependency theory, racialized democracy, and world-market hierarchy.
7. **Greenspan interpretation** — intention/effect remain insufficiently distinguished.
8. **Terminology** — capacity / utilization / potential output / output gap still require a full audit.

---

# 8. Editorial rule for using the HOPE reports

The HOPE reports are not instructions to turn the paper into a HOPE article.

They are diagnostic evidence.

For the ROPE revision:

- preserve the non-reductionist political-economy ambition praised by Referee 2;
- preserve and elevate the survey/institutional material praised by Referee 1;
- resolve the common objections about agency, evidence, continuity, and explanatory overreach;
- shift the final payoff toward the contemporary heterodox utilization debate.

The prior rejection should therefore be used as a **falsification test**:
a problem identified independently by both HOPE referees should be presumed real unless the current manuscript clearly demonstrates that it has been repaired.
