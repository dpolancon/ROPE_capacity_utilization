# 1. Paper identity

This is a **historical political economy of macroeconomic measurement** paper with a direct payoff for **heterodox macroeconomics and the capacity-utilization controversy**.

It is **not** primarily:

- a general history of American Keynesianism;
- a general history of neoliberalism;
- a complete regulation-theory account of Fordism/post-Fordism;
- a history of Bretton Woods;
- a history of U.S. racial politics;
- a dependency-theory paper;
- a decolonizing-economics paper;
- an STS paper;
- a paper claiming capacity utilization caused the neoliberal transition.

Those literatures may appear only where they clarify the locked argument.

---

# 2. Explanandum

The article explains:

> **How competing conceptions and measurements of capacity utilization acquired unequal institutional authority in the United States, and how the policy meaning of the institutionalized utilization apparatus changed as postwar demand management entered crisis.**

The explanandum is **the construction, institutionalization, and reinterpretation of capacity-utilization measurement**.

The explanandum is **not the rise and fall of American Keynesianism as a whole**.

American Keynesianism and its crisis provide the historical regime within which the measurement object is constructed and reinterpreted.

---

# 3. Primary claim — LOCKED

> **Capacity utilization did not become a postwar macroeconomic policy indicator because a theoretically self-evident measure was discovered. Competing engineering, survey-based, cost-based, and physical approaches operationalized productive capacity differently. Survey-based measures acquired official authority through a historically specific combination of administrative usability, institutional reach, path dependence, and compatibility with prevailing policy routines.**

Implications of this claim:

- do not reduce adoption to “methodological superiority”;
- do not reduce adoption to “politics alone”;
- institutional authority is the outcome to explain;
- alternative measures matter because they reveal that the object was contestable.

This is the article's principal empirical-historical contribution.

---

# 4. Secondary claim — LOCKED

> **During the crisis of postwar demand management, the policy meaning attached to high utilization changed. Utilization increasingly functioned as evidence of inflationary constraint rather than primarily as evidence of mobilizable slack. This shift is best understood as a reorientation of an existing technocratic/statistical infrastructure rather than either a completely clean rupture or the literal survival of an unchanged indicator.**

This claim requires a carefully defined unit of continuity.

The preferred unit is:

> **interpretive and institutional infrastructure / policy-use regime**

—not necessarily an identical statistical series.

Do not write “the same apparatus” unless the noun “apparatus” has been defined in this sense.

---

# 5. Contemporary ROPE payoff — LOCKED

> **The history of capacity-utilization measurement matters for contemporary heterodox macroeconomics because the observable properties of an official utilization series may partly reflect the institutional choices through which the series was defined, benchmarked, smoothed, revised, and given policy authority. Therefore, disputes over the normal rate of utilization cannot treat measurement as theoretically neutral.**

This payoff must appear in:

- the introduction;
- the conclusion;
- the abstract if space permits.

It must not be saved for the conclusion.

The paper does **not** need to take a side in the endogenous-versus-exogenous normal-utilization controversy.

Its contribution is prior to that choice: it problematizes the measurement object on which empirical adjudication relies.

---

# 6. Political-economy implication — BOUNDED

Allowed formulation:

> **The reinterpretation of high utilization as an inflationary constraint participated in a wider reorientation of macroeconomic governance in which maintaining price stability increasingly limited the policy space assigned to full-employment demand management.**

This is a political-economy implication consistent with the historical evidence.

Stronger formulations are **not authorized without direct evidence**, including:

- “capacity utilization was an instrument of labor discipline”;
- “the index was used to defeat organized labor”;
- “financial capital recoded the indicator”;
- “the purpose of the measurement change was to suppress egalitarian claims”;
- “capacity utilization caused the neoliberal turn”;
- “the Federal Reserve redesigned the measure in order to discipline distribution.”

Such claims may be restored only if actor-specific documentary evidence establishes the mechanism.

---

# 7. Agency rule — LOCKED

The paper must distinguish:

1. **intent** — what historical actors sought to do;
2. **action** — what actors actually changed or implemented;
3. **effect** — what an intervention did in an institutional/discursive setting;
4. **compatibility** — how a measurement practice fit a broader policy regime.

Never infer (1) from (3) or (4).

Permitted verbs when evidence is limited:
- enabled;
- facilitated;
- aligned with;
- became compatible with;
- was interpreted as;
- contributed to;
- participated in;
- reinforced.

Higher-burden verbs:
- designed to;
- intended to;
- weaponized;
- disciplined;
- suppressed;
- defeated;
- strategically selected in order to.

Use high-burden verbs only with actor-specific evidence.

---

# 8. Continuity / discontinuity rule — LOCKED

The transition from postwar Keynesianism to rules/credibility-oriented macroeconomics must be presented as **structured continuity plus substantive reorientation**.

Potential continuities to demonstrate:
- reliance on formalized expert macroeconomic reasoning;
- continued authority of technocratic institutions;
- continued need to operationalize an unobservable/contested notion of economic constraint;
- use of quantitative indicators to delimit permissible policy action.

Potential discontinuities to demonstrate:
- slack/full-employment orientation versus inflation/natural-rate constraint;
- discretionary stabilization versus rules/credibility;
- observable idle resources versus model-implied equilibrium/potential benchmarks;
- different political implications of what counts as “normal” or sustainable operation.

Do not use “same toolkit” as a shortcut for this analysis.

# 10. Framework rule

## Institutional analysis
**Primary explanatory mode.**

Ask:
- who produced the measure?
- how was it defined?
- who used it?
- through which organizational routine?
- why did one measure become administratively authoritative?
- what changed in its policy interpretation?

## History of economic thought
**Supporting mode.**

Use Hansen, Samuelson, Lucas, Sargent, Klein, etc. only insofar as their ideas:
- changed what policymakers wanted to measure;
- changed how constraints were conceptualized;
- changed how utilization evidence was interpreted.

## Regulation theory
**Contextual mode only.**

Fordism/post-Fordism may periodize the macro-historical setting.
Do not require the reader to accept regulation theory for the measurement argument to work.

## STS / sociology of quantification
**Conceptual background only unless explicitly reopened.**

The paper may use “socio-technical device” / “measurement infrastructure” but should not claim an STS contribution requiring a comprehensive STS literature review.

# 12. Mechanism chain — REQUIRED

The revised article should be able to document this chain:

### Phase A — Construction
Competing definitions of productive capacity emerge.

### Phase B — Institutionalization
Survey-based measures become regular, administratively usable, and embedded in official routines.

### Phase C — Contestation
Alternative physical/economic measures expose conceptual and empirical differences.

### Phase D — Crisis and reinterpretation
Stagflation and changing macroeconomic theory alter what policymakers seek from a capacity indicator.

### Phase E — Reorientation
High utilization increasingly signifies an inflationary constraint / proximity to a sustainable limit rather than primarily unused mobilizable resources.

### Phase F — Contemporary implication
Because the series embodies institutional and conceptual choices, empirical disputes over normal utilization must interrogate the measure itself.

Every major section must advance at least one phase of this chain.

# 18. Definition of “ideological embeddedness”

The phrase must not mean simply “biased” or “political.”

Preferred working definition:

> **A macroeconomic indicator is ideologically embedded when its operational definition, institutional stabilization, and policy interpretation depend on historically specific assumptions about what economic state is normal, observable, and actionable.**

This definition is compatible with the paper's non-reductionist ambition.

It does not require claiming that statistical producers consciously manipulate data for class purposes.

Use consistently or replace the title concept.

---

# 19. Non-reductionism lock

The paper must preserve one of the strengths identified by HOPE Referee 2: it should not reduce economists' work to class interests.

Therefore:

- ideas have relative autonomy;
- technical disputes are real;
- measurement alternatives can differ for genuinely methodological reasons;
- institutions select/use knowledge under practical and political constraints;
- political consequences do not prove political intent.

This is not a retreat from political economy.
It is the condition for making the political-economy argument credible.
